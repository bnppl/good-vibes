# JPMorgan Chase — detailed AI-assisted development research

[Profile](index.md) · [Source register](sources.md) · [Workflow](workflow.md) · [Slides](slides.md)

## Scope and reading guide

Gap-first public-source study; recommendations and recruitment statements are not observed development workflows. The question is how software is built using AI, not how an end-user AI feature is designed. Start with the concrete case; use the claims below for the shared twelve-dimension comparison.

## What the public record establishes

The bank’s careers material states that development roles use internal AI tools. It does not supply a dated, inspectable coding task, its inputs, executed checks or approval path. The page therefore remains context rather than support for a current-window operational claim. [S01](sources.md#s01)

The March DEV UP recap is a dated primary source that discusses platform choices, explicit context and the intended direction of development. Its date was checked in the page’s `datePublished` metadata. It is a conference recap: an advocated destination must not become evidence that a particular platform was deployed everywhere. [S02](sources.md#s02)

## A forecast is not a case study

The March technology report discusses emerging trends. It can explain strategic vocabulary, but cannot establish the bank’s actual coding agent, test runner, review boundaries or measurable return. [S03](sources.md#s03)

## Historical detail stays historical

The April 2025 Payments developer article supplies a more concrete tooling account, but predates the cutoff. Senatus is associated with 2022 research and likewise cannot fill a 2026 workflow. Neither receives a new practice date just because it was re-read today. [S04](sources.md#s04) [S05](sources.md#s05)

## Search boundary and missing evidence

This review searched the bank’s technology and Payments developer domains for coding assistants, AI development, internal engineering and 2026 cases; inspected the conference recap, trend report and adoption page; and screened historical technical leads. No detailed recent end-to-end primary workflow was established. This is a bounded result, not a claim that no internal workflow or other publication exists.

A stronger future record would name a team and task, describe what context an agent receives, show what it can execute, identify approval/merge/release authority, and report quality and cost alongside output. This cache deliberately leaves those fields unknown. The bank’s open-source profile and FINOS participation do not close that evidence gap. [S06](sources.md#s06)
