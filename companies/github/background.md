## Reported practice

One Applied Science team account describes planning with Copilot, agent implementation, automated review loops and final human review. It also describes human-controlled regression contracts and ongoing code/documentation cleanup.

[Primary source: Agent-driven development in Copilot Applied Science](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/agent-driven-development-in-copilot-applied-science/)

## What makes this a useful engineering study

Agent-friendly repositories with independent contracts and human review. The open-source link provides a concrete place to inspect code, contribution practices and engineering artifacts. It is background for selection, not proof that this AI workflow produced that project or that every team works identically.

## Limits

This is a named team and project, not a GitHub-wide mandate. The fact that the resulting software involves agents is incidental; the qualifying evidence is how it was developed.

Practice dates are those described in the source, not the date of this review. Exact task dates and the complete current private setup are not established unless stated. These accounts were inspected, not independently executed. Open-source origin, ongoing maintenance and project governance are different relationships; foundation/community projects are labelled accordingly.

## Questions for a deeper comparison

- What intent or contract does the agent receive, and who can change it?
- What executes independently to check the result?
- Who owns review, merge and production release decisions?
- What changed in defect rate, lead time, operational outcomes and cost per completed task?

A product AI feature, a tool capability demo or an adoption percentage alone cannot answer those questions.
