# Research how a company builds software using AI

Inputs: COMPANY, optional COMPARE_WITH, PUBLICATIONS_SINCE (default 2025-10-01), AS_OF (default today), TEAM_SCOPE, STAGE (research/slides/compare/all) and REFRESH (default false).

Read `docs/company-cache-schema.md` for the storage contract, the twelve dimensions, claim/date fields and slide anatomy. Use existing company directories and identities. The research subject is how people use AI to develop, verify and maintain software. AI features in an end-user product are not evidence of an AI-assisted development workflow.

## Separate research from derived views

- Research creates a missing detailed cache. Refresh existing research only if requested. Seek original authored engineering cases, practitioner transcripts, dated/pinned repository artifacts and studies of actual internal development. Existing introductory profiles are leads; a complete cache needs research and source findings.
- Slides read the existing cache without browsing, preserving its inspection dates. Lead with real cases and observed failures. Keep prompts or optional labs visibly separate from reported practice. Preserve card IDs and company-specific storage keys.
- Comparison reads the same cached claims, with no new research dates or independent facts. Missing research is a prerequisite, not permission to invent a method. Explicitly identify material cutoff/scope differences.
- All ensures selected caches exist, then derives the requested views. A comparison without an explicit research stage defaults to cached evidence.

## Research questions

Trace the workflow from intent to deployment. Look for actual inputs, task slicing, planning, context, tools, tests, reviews, permission boundaries, integration checks, production signals and rollback authority. Seek failures and exceptions, rather than collecting adoption claims alone. Explain why a team chose its approach and which prerequisites made it work.

Use recent publications for active fields. Separate publication/revision dates, the dates of reported practice and actual inspection. A recent retrospective can describe older work. A living handbook needs a verified revision; a changed display date cannot make every passage newly written. Record a repository commit when inspected and useful. Keep older OSS contributions and books as background.

Attribute each statement to its actual team, project, author and evidence type. A CTO personal experiment is not a company-wide policy. A public policy is not observed compliance. A vendor customer case remains a vendor-framed report. An inspected artifact is not a successful executed run. Industry aggregate telemetry cannot establish its publisher's internal workflow. Parent/subsidiary companies are not independent samples.

Record all twelve comparison dimensions, leaving unsupported ones explicitly unknown. Mirror positions, labels and qualifications in the visible index and research chapter. Every active field links to the original source and a cached claim with provenance. Explain limits on company-wide scope, publication eligibility, inaccessible recordings and exact practice dates.

## Derive useful lessons

Compare concrete engineering decisions, not company reputations or fictional numerical ratings. Match task types and observation boundaries before comparing outcomes. Distinguish implementation autonomy, automatic review, approval, merge and production release. Treat PR volume and tokens as activity; use defect/revert rates, accepted changes, lead time, customer impact, incident outcomes and cost per useful completed task when available.

For optional hands-on practice, choose one bounded task and write independently observable acceptance conditions. Have an agent propose a patch, validate it, inspect the evidence and record who decides to merge. This is our proposed exercise, not a company's undocumented procedure. Do not execute implementations or publish changes merely to explain the exercise.

Review organisation/industry scope and ensure product capabilities have not become claims of internal use. Preserve unrelated files and reading metadata. Report outputs and remaining limits.
