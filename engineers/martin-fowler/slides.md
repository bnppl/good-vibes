## Card 09

### A context interview, not an invented coding session

> **Ask the model to interview you before you ask it to build.**
>
> Personal use · published 2026-05-14. His 14 May 2026 Interrogatory LLM entry reports trying an interview to assemble context and needing repeated reminders about one-question pacing. It credits Harper Reed. The useful observed technique is eliciting and reviewing context; a complete personal implementation-to-release sequence is not documented here. His September reflection adds direct experience of useful answers mixed with confident fabrication.

**In the wild:** Practice period: Trial before publication; exact date not stated.

**Takeaway:** Distinguish the reported work from a complete daily workflow.

## Card 01

### Keep the code in view

> **Delegating the typing does not delegate responsibility.**
>
> Recommendation · published 2026-05-21. Fowler describes agentic engineering as work where people still care about generated code, its structure, and the feedback around it. This is a conceptual recommendation, not a complete private workflow.

**In the wild:** Published recommendation: his May entry lists code review, test results and other feedback as human evaluation inputs; it is not a log of his own session.

**Takeaway:** Keep a human accountable for what the software does and how it works.

[Optional practice: adapt locally](research.md) · [Recent evidence](recent-ai.md) · [Primary source](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/AgenticEngineering.html)

## Card 02

### Let the model ask

> **The missing context may be an unanswered question.**
>
> Personal use · published 2026-05-14. His interrogatory-LLM entry describes interviews that create or review context documents. He credits Harper Reed and reports that his own trial needed reminders about one-question pacing.

**In the wild:** Reported trial: he needed to remind the interviewing model to ask one question at a time.

**Takeaway:** Review the generated context report against the answers.

## Card 03

### Concrete before abstract

> **A scenario can expose what a diagram hides.**
>
> Recommendation · published 2026-01-21. In a multi-person conversation, Fowler’s own contributions favour concrete scenarios and domain-familiar abstractions. Do not assign every speaker’s claims to him.

**In the wild:** Published conversation: his own turns discuss scenarios and domain understanding; the other speakers’ claims remain theirs.

**Takeaway:** Let observed cases sharpen the model.
